ITC Recon Desk

I designed this tool for small-business accountants. It checks their purchase register against GSTR-2B, shows how much Input Tax Credit is at risk before GSTR-3B is due, and tells them which vendors to follow up with first.

1 · Executive summary

Why I picked this problem

During my finance internship I spent many hours matching records line by line in Excel. Later, as a Business Analyst, I reviewed accounting software like Tally, QuickBooks and Zoho, and I kept noticing the same gap. These tools are good at recording purchases, but they do little to help an accountant work out which purchases will actually get tax credit. I wanted to see how far careful requirements work and a simple set of matching rules could close that gap.

Here is the problem. A business can only claim Input Tax Credit (ITC) on purchase invoices its suppliers have reported, which appear in the auto-drafted GSTR-2B statement. Most small businesses check this by hand in Excel, using VLOOKUP between their purchase register and a 2B download. Invoice numbers are written differently on each side, GSTINs get typed wrong, and vendors file late. Mismatches are found after GSTR-3B is filed, or not at all, and the business either loses credit or pays interest on a reversal.

Objective

Cut monthly reconciliation from days of manual matching to a reviewed exception list, and show ITC at risk before the GSTR-3B due date.

What I made

The full set of BA documents I would hand to a product team: research, personas, process maps, BRD, FRD, user stories, a traceability matrix and UAT cases. I also built a working prototype so the rules could be tested rather than only described.

2 · Research

Context, method and hypotheses

Regulatory context

  • Section 16(2)(aa) of the CGST Act and Rule 36(4) limit ITC to invoices the supplier has reported, as reflected in GSTR-2B.
  • GSTR-2B is a static, monthly statement built from suppliers' GSTR-1 / IFF filings.
  • Since October 2024 the Invoice Management System (IMS) lets the recipient accept, reject or keep each supplier invoice pending before 2B is finalised. That makes early, invoice-level review more valuable.
  • After the September 2025 rate rationalisation, most goods sit in the 5% and 18% slabs, which the sample data reflects.

Primary sources: gst.gov.in and cbic-gst.gov.in.

Method

  • Desk research: GST portal advisories, CBIC rules, and feature reviews of Tally, Zoho Books, QuickBooks and ClearTax-style tools (from my product-review work at Meru Accounting).
  • Data simulation: a 40-vendor, three-month purchase register with error patterns seen in practice (format drift, typos, late filers).
  • Gap analysis: mapped what accountants do by hand against what tools automate.
  • Next step: I plan to interview 6–8 MSME accountants and CA-firm staff using the guide below. Until then I am treating the personas as educated guesses, not facts.

Interview guide (excerpt)

  1. Walk me through the last time you reconciled purchases with 2B. Which files, which steps?
  2. How long did it take, and which part took longest?
  3. What do you do when an invoice is in your books but not in 2B?
  4. How do you track which vendors you have already reminded?
  5. On what date do you usually finish, compared with the GSTR-3B due date?
  6. If one screen could show you only one thing, what would it be?

4 · Process analysis

AS-IS and TO-BE

AS-IS (manual)

flowchart TD
  A[Export purchase register from Tally] --> B[Download GSTR-2B Excel]
  B --> C[Clean invoice numbers by hand]
  C --> D[VLOOKUP on invoice number]
  D --> E{Found?}
  E -- Yes --> F[Eyeball tax amount]
  E -- No --> G[Search manually in 2B]
  G --> H[Note in a side sheet]
  F --> I[File GSTR-3B]
  H --> I
  I --> J[Chase vendors by memory]

Pain points: format cleaning is manual, value differences are checked by eye, vendor follow-up has no owner, and the work finishes close to or after the due date.

TO-BE (ITC Recon Desk)

flowchart TD
  A[Upload register and 2B CSV] --> B[Validate GSTINs and columns]
  B --> C[Normalise invoice numbers]
  C --> D[Match on GSTIN + invoice]
  D --> E[Classify exceptions with tolerance]
  E --> F[ITC-at-risk summary]
  E --> G[Vendor follow-up list]
  F --> H[Review and act in IMS]
  G --> H
  H --> I[File GSTR-3B by the 20th]

Gains: rules replace manual cleaning, every exception has a type and an action, and risk is visible before filing.

6 · Business Requirements Document (summary)

BRD

Business objectives

  • BO-1 Identify ITC at risk before the GSTR-3B due date every month.
  • BO-2 Reduce manual reconciliation effort by at least 70%.
  • BO-3 Give every exception an owner and a next action.
  • BO-4 Recover credit for supplier invoices that were never booked.

Scope

In scope: B2B purchase invoices, CSV import of register and GSTR-2B, matching, exception classification, vendor summary.

Out of scope (v1): credit/debit notes, imports (IMPG), direct GSTN API connection, e-invoice IRN validation, auto-filing.

Business rules

IDRuleException raised
BR-01Invoice numbers are compared after normalisation: uppercase, split on symbols and spaces, leading zeros removed from each part.None (enables match)
BR-02Same GSTIN and invoice, tax difference within tolerance (default ₹1).Matched
BR-03Same GSTIN and invoice, tax difference above tolerance. Excess claimed counts as ITC at risk.Value mismatch
BR-04Matched, but the 2B period is later than the books month. Credit is available in the later period.Period mismatch
BR-05Same invoice and tax, GSTIN differs by 1–2 characters.GSTIN mismatch
BR-06Same GSTIN, invoice differs by one character, tax within tolerance. Needs human review.Probable match
BR-07Invoice in books, nothing in 2B. Full tax is at risk.Missing in 2B
BR-08Invoice in 2B, not in books. Credit may be unclaimed.Missing in books
BR-09Same GSTIN and invoice booked more than once.Duplicate in books
BR-10GSTIN must pass format and check-digit validation.Flag on the row

Assumptions

  • Users can export the purchase register and 2B to CSV.
  • One GSTIN per legal entity per state.
  • The CA or tax consultant signs off tolerance values.

Constraints

  • Taxpayer data stays on the user's machine in v1 (data privacy).
  • Must handle 5,000 invoices a month without noticeable delay.
  • Outputs must be explainable to an auditor: every status links to a rule ID.

Non-functional requirements

  • NFR-01 Performance: 5,000 × 5,000 rows reconciled in under 3 seconds on a standard laptop.
  • NFR-02 Privacy: no upload to a server in v1.
  • NFR-03 Auditability: each result shows its rule ID and both source values.
  • NFR-04 Usability: a first-time user completes a run in under 5 minutes without training.

12 · Reflection

What I learned

Rules beat guesswork

When I wrote every matching rule down with an ID, the gaps became obvious. One example: what happens when the invoice matches but the GSTIN is one character off? Writing it down forced a decision.

Tests keep the documents honest

Linking each user story to a test case that actually runs showed me which requirements were vague. If I couldn't write a test for a story, the story wasn't finished.

The user wants a to-do list

I started out planning a big dashboard. Thinking through the accountant's month changed my mind: what they need most is a short list of which vendors to chase, in order.
